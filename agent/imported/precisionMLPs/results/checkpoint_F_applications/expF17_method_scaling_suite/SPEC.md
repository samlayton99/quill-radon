# expF17 -- clean cross-method scaling suite (SPEC)

**Status: spec recorded from Sam 2026-08-31; decisions returned same day. Step 0 (halo) DONE. PINN arm DEFERRED at Sam's instruction. Not otherwise started.**

Supersedes the reading of Catherine's `cdeng/expF15`/`expF16` benchmark, which was found to be confounded (unequal dictionaries, unequal information, unequal tuning, oracle selection on the scored set, and two reference oracles above machine epsilon).

## 1. Purpose

Apples-to-apples scaling comparison of PINN-style representations at matched hidden-unit count, on a task suite with machine-precision oracles, under two regimes: fitting a known oracle, and solving the PDE without one.

## 2. Global rules

- **tanh everywhere.**
- **No polynomial block** in the baseline runs (Plots 1 and 2). Polynomials appear only in Plot 3.
- **Nothing selected on the test set.** Hyperparameters chosen on a held-out validation split; test error reported once. (This is the specific flaw in the prior benchmark.)
- **Equal tuning effort per method**, declared up front as a fixed grid size, not per-cell search.
- Every reported number carries error bars over 3 seeds.
- Shared axis scales across subplots; single legend at the top; task families share a colour cue in the subplot title.
- Legends outside the axes (house rule).

## 3. Methods

**Plot 1 axis (single hidden layer, N units, directly comparable):**
| method | inner weights | readout |
|---|---|---|
| QI-Radon | from theory | solved |
| QI-tensor | from theory (tensor product) | solved |
| ELM / random features | random | solved |
| 1-layer tanh PINN | trained (Adam -> L-BFGS -> NNCG) | trained |
| direct Fourier | integer frequencies, 2K+1 ~ N | solved |

Direct Fourier is always **computed and stored** but gated behind a plotting flag so it can be excluded from the figure without recomputation.

**Plot 2 only (not on the N axis):** BWLer, RBF, Chebyshev pseudospectral.

## 4. Domain and geometry matching (Sam's requirement)

- QI-tensor's square and QI-Radon's disk must cover **the same area**.
- The **halo/collar delta must match** between them.
- The **inner test region** must have the same area for both.
- Test metric: rel L2 on the inner region. QI-tensor scores on its own (square) version of that region; every other method uses the disk/inner region.

## 5. Scale axis

- Widths: **64, 128, 256, 512, 1024**, extensible later without recompute.
- Data:width ratio: **oversample 4x-5x collocation rows per neuron** (expF03 default; expH05 used 8x; expE01 used >2x). Pick one and hold it.
- Seeds: **3**. Generated by translating every data point by a small random epsilon vector (~1e-10), holding the data otherwise constant, plus 1-2 extra data points per additional seed. Extra seeds queueable.

## 6. The two regimes (the main axis)

- **Plot 1a -- oracle.** Fit the known closed-form solution directly. No PDE.
- **Plot 1b -- dynamic PDE solve.** No closed form assumed; solve as a PINN would. Use **expF03's winning nonlinear recipe** for both QI-Radon and QI-tensor: coarse-to-fine **cascade init** (converge at W/4, refit, polish), damped Gauss-Newton with backtracking on the stacked residual, flat lambda=0.25, rcond=1e-15. Same procedure for both, no extra tuning for either.

## 7. Tasks

The expF13 suite (convection c40/c80, reaction, wave, Burgers, Poisson-manufactured), plus **Darcy flow and other FNO tasks**.

## 11. Step 0 RESULT -- the halo (done, 2026-08-31)

Sam's recollection was correct and understated. Sweeping halo x N x target on the lstsq readout (5 targets, N in {32..1024}, halo in {0..70}, lambda=0.25, oversample 4x, rel L2 on [-1,1] including the boundary):

- **halo = 8 is sufficient everywhere.** Errors at halo 8, 14, 30 and 70 agree to within a small factor in every cell.
- **The requirement is FLAT IN N** -- it does not grow.
- `default_halo(1024, 0.25) = 409`, so the current default is **51x over** at N=1024. Cells that plateau above the floor (runge N<=64, multiscale N=32, highfreq N=32) are flat across the entire halo range -- resolution limits, not halo limits.
- Prior evidence: expG01 saw halo>=5 hold the floor for sine at N=128; expF03 only tested halo=0 vs default ("load-bearing but binary") so it never probed the middle.

Figure: `halo_requirement.png`. Data: scratch `halo_sweep.json`.

**Consequence 1 -- the matched quantity is COUNT, not delta.** Because the requirement is flat in N, the invariant is the halo *count* (equivalently ~2 tanh transition widths, since 8 centers x lambda=0.25 = 2). In distance it shrinks as 16/N. Radon's collar of 1.6 at W=1024 works out to ~7 extra offsets per direction -- already the same magnitude, which is a good consistency check. So match Radon and tensor on halo COUNT.

**Consequence 2 -- BLOCKING: the tensor cannot be built below N ~ 1024.** With N hidden units total, the 2-D tensor gets sqrt(N) centers per axis, and halo 8 per side consumes 16 of them:

| N | centers/axis | halo | interior | viable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 8 | 16 | -8 | NO |
| 256 | 16 | 16 | 0 | NO |
| 1024 | 32 | 16 | 16 | marginal |
| 4096 | 64 | 16 | 48 | yes |
| 16384 | 128 | 16 | 112 | yes |

The width ladder 64-1024 therefore gives QI-tensor at most one usable point. **Recommend extending the ladder upward (4096, 16384) so the tensor has a curve.** This is a real result in its own right -- the halo cost compounds across axes -- not a bug.


## 12. Step 0c RESULT -- the halo is FREE (final policy, 2026-08-31)

Sam's decision: the halo is added ON TOP of the interior budget, never displacing interior
resolution. Interior = sqrt(W) nodes per line in both geometries. Test region back to r = 1
(the full region). Match on the CALCULATED total, i.e. each method is plotted at its true
total column count.

Measured (4 targets, interior W in {128,256,512,1024}, halo 0-12, lambda swept):

- **Radon saturates by halo 6**; 6 through 12 are flat to within floor noise.
- **The tensor keeps gaining a little to 12**, driven entirely by the oblique-ridge target
  (W=1024: 2.6e-11 at h=4 -> 1.2e-12 at h=8 -> 3.4e-13 at h=12), i.e. by the corner centers.
- **halo = 0 is catastrophic** for both (1e-7 to 1e-2). halo 2 buys 2-3 orders; 4-6 reaches the floor.
- **Sam's 8 / max 12 is correct under the free-halo framing.** The earlier `line/6`
  recommendation was an artifact of the fixed-budget sweep, where the halo displaced interior
  resolution and the curve turned back up. Under a free halo the curve is monotone-then-flat.

**Adopted: halo = 8 per side, both geometries.** Radon is indifferent above 6, and 8 keeps the
tensor within about one order of its best on the one target that still cares. A larger halo
would only shift both rightward on a column-based x-axis for no accuracy gain on Radon.

**Column accounting (structural, not bookkeeping).** Radon = n^2 + 2nh; tensor = n^2 + 4nh + 4h^2.
Radon's parameter space is (periodic angle) x (bounded offset), so only ONE coordinate has a
boundary and needs a halo. The tensor's is (bounded x) x (bounded y) -- two boundaries, hence two
strips plus four corners (the 4h^2 term). At interior W=1024, halo 8: Radon 1537 columns,
tensor 2401. This is why the two cannot simultaneously match on interior resolution, halo count,
AND columns. Resolution: plot each at its true total column count and let the points land where
they land.

**W = 128 is below resolution** (Radon sep_sine 2e-2) and W = 256 leaves Radon's oblique target at
4e-6 -- that is the expH05 direction cliff reproducing, since 16 spokes cannot align with a
36.9-degree ridge. Recommend the ladder start at 256 and read 256 as a cliff datapoint, not a
scaling point.

Figure: `halo_free.png`. Data: scratch `halo_free.json`.

poisson_man (residual ~1e-50). Burgers: **Cole-Hopf, 75 Gauss-Hermite nodes -> 2.7e-15**,

landed in `problems.py` and the "No closed form" docstring corrected. Darcy-original is the
single acknowledged reference-capped exception (accuracy still unmeasured).

## 13.3 Coordinates -- no per-task translation needed
Every task is posed in scaled P = (xi, eta) in [-1,1]^2 with the physical map folded into the
operator coefficients: convection/reaction x = pi(xi+1), t = (eta+1)/2; wave x = (xi+1)/2;
Burgers x = xi; poisson (x,y) = P/2; Darcy [0,1]^2 -> [-1,1]^2. xi is always space, eta always
time. The geometry code never needs to know which task it is running.
**Exceptions:** poisson_man is the square MINUS four holes (mask with `in_poisson_domain`; the
tensor wastes ~13% of its centres in dead space -- report it separately, not averaged in).
Burgers eval grid moves to the 200x200 uniform grid the time problems use.

## 13.4 Regions
**Train on [-1,1]^2. Score on [-1,1]^2. Halo outside both.** Measured: training out to the halo
edge is 9x to 300,000x WORSE (density-matched), because the dictionary spends capacity on a
region nobody scores; it is also impossible in the dynamic arm (no PDE outside Omega), and
PINN_FEASIBILITY.md:11 lists "the halo needs no data outside Omega" as a PDE-specific bonus.
**ALSO report the inscribed-disk (r=1) error separately** -- see 13.10.

## 13.5 Geometry
- **Radon**: directions theta_j = pi*j/J (endpoint rule, axes included). Per-spoke extent
  +-T(theta) with T = |cos|+|sin| (the square's support function). **Uniform offset spacing h
  across all spokes** (offset count varies as 2T/h), single gamma = lam/h. Offsets symmetric
  about t=0, so every spoke's best-resolved band runs through the square's centre. Replaces the
  flat collar of 1.6, which wasted ~20% of offsets on average and 37% on the axis directions.
- **Tensor**: n_ax centres per axis over [-1,1], symmetric about the origin.
- **Exact column match**: J = n_ax, M = n_ax+2, +1 bias = (n_ax+1)^2 = tensor at n_ax/axis.
  Under uniform spacing this becomes sum_j(2T(theta_j)/h + 1 + 2*halo), matched by bisection on h.
- Halo cost is structural, not bookkeeping: Radon n^2+2nh (one periodic coordinate, one bounded);
  tensor n^2+4nh+4h^2 (two bounded coordinates, hence corners). Report column counts alongside.

## 13.6 Halo
Default `halo = min(n_ax/8, 8)` per side. **Per-method, validation-selected over {h0-2, h0, h0+2}**
-- identical effort, method-specific value, selected h recorded. Rationale: equal tuning EFFORT is
the fairness criterion, not equal hyperparameter VALUES (nobody forces tanh and gaussian to share
a lambda). Evidence for the default: at fixed columns the acceptable band intersection across both
geometries x four sizes is exactly {0.125}; at n_ax=64 it is exactly {8}, where the fraction branch
meets the independently measured 1-D count threshold. `default_halo`'s floor of 70 comes from the
mpmath convolution tail and does NOT apply to the lstsq path (51x over at N=1024).

## 13.7 Bandwidth, solver, data
- lambda from expC07's closed-form rule (kernel Fourier tail at the first grid harmonic = eps):
  **tanh 0.25, gaussian-RBF 0.53, ELM 0.25**. Fourier/Chebyshev/BWLer have no bandwidth.
- **rcond = 1e-15.** expF13 measured 1e-13 costing 35x on wave (`run.py:59`); expF03's cascade
  winner was also 1e-15. Truncated lstsq/SVD only -- **never form Phi^T Phi** (expA01: ~7 decades).
- **Data: exactly 4x the column count, shuffled** for numerical randomness.
- All hyperparameter selection on a held-out VALIDATION split, never the test set.

## 13.8 Scale
Widths (columns): **128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048.** 4096 and 8192 queued as extras -- note that at
2401 columns wave is at 7.5e-10 and convection 1.8e-9, and expF13 needed W=9216 for wave to reach
1.6e-13, so **this ladder measures descent slopes, not floors**. The bottom two rungs are
direction-cliff-dominated for Radon (~11 and ~15 spokes) and should not have a scaling law drawn
through them.

## 13.9 Seeds and statistics
3 seeds (more queueable). **Seed = grid-phase jitter + collocation RNG**: Radon direction phase
in [0, pi/J) and offset phase; tensor grid-origin shift per axis. These perturb the DICTIONARY,
never the problem, so they are legal for space-time and anisotropic operators. **No rotation**
(does not survive square domains or space-time). **No 1e-10 data jitter** (measures rounding noise
for a deterministic geometry and would fake-shrink ELM/PINN bars). ELM and the PINN get random init.
**Geometric mean everywhere; error bars = geometric standard deviation, [g/s_g, g*s_g].**

## 13.10 NEW -- the Radon angular field of view (Crowther), and dual scoring
With J views at offset spacing h, a Radon reconstruction is faithful only inside radius
**R_max = J*h/pi**. Measured across the whole ladder under the balanced J=M split:

| cols | n_ax | halo | n_int | h | J | R_max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 10 | 1 | 8 | 0.364 | 10 | 1.158 |
| 256 | 15 | 2 | 11 | 0.255 | 15 | 1.216 |
| 512 | 22 | 3 | 16 | 0.170 | 22 | 1.189 |
| 1024 | 31 | 4 | 23 | 0.116 | 31 | 1.142 |
| 2048 | 44 | 6 | 32 | 0.082 | 44 | 1.150 |

The square's inradius is 1.0 and its circumradius is sqrt(2) = 1.414, so **the corners (21.5% of
the area) are angularly undersampled at every width -- and R_max is scale-invariant** (J ~ n and
h ~ 1/n, so J*h does not grow with budget). Radon therefore carries a fixed corner penalty on a
square that NO amount of budget removes under a balanced split. R_max scales with the aspect ratio
J/M, not the total; reaching the corners needs **J/M ~ 1.23**.

Consequences, all three adopted:
1. **Score on the full square (headline) AND on the inscribed disk r=1 (secondary).** The square
   keeps the IC/BC in the metric and stays comparable to expF13; the disk isolates Radon's real
   capability. Reporting both makes the corner effect attributable instead of silently capping
   Radon's curve and being misread as a method limit.
2. Keep balanced J=M as the headline split (the tensor is structurally symmetric; letting Radon
   optimise a split the tensor cannot access breaks apples-to-apples).
3. Add **two extra Radon lines**: J/M = 1.23 (corner-corrected) and the expH05-optimal split
   (M* ~ B^0.27..0.44), so what balance costs Radon is visible in the same plot.

# 14. THE RECIPE (2026-09-01) -- supersedes 13.10 and the method list in 13.1

## 14.1 Crowther is REJECTED; balanced J=M stands
I proposed sizing Radon so its resolved disk (R_max = J*h/pi) covers the square's circumradius,
giving J/M ~ 1.3. **Measured, and it is 9-15x WORSE at the budget that matters.** At C=2048:

| J | M | J/M | R_max | convection | wave | reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 38 | 54 | 0.70 | 0.75 | 1.28e-08 | 1.76e-10 | 1.60e-05 |
| 45 | 46 | 0.98 | 1.11 | **1.14e-09** | **3.54e-10** | **1.94e-06** |
| 52 | 39 | 1.33 | 1.51 | 1.00e-08 | 5.46e-09 | 1.48e-06 |
| 68 | 30 | 2.27 | 2.62 | 8.53e-06 | 7.92e-06 | 1.24e-05 |

J/M=1.33 satisfies Crowther (R_max >= sqrt2) and still loses to balanced, which "fails" it.
Crowther describes angular aliasing when INVERTING measured projection data; a least-squares fit
in a ridge dictionary has no hard field of view. Borrowed theory, does not transfer.
Consistent with expH05 (alpha = 0.27-0.44, directions growing slower than sqrt(B)).
**Balanced J = M is the default**, the exact-column identity is unchanged, and no second scoring
region is needed. Note the regime dependence: at C=512 the optimum IS J/M=1.3 -- more directions
help when resolution-starved, hurt once resolving. Hence J/M is a KNOB, not a constant.

## 14.2 One recipe for every (task, method, budget, seed)
1. Build the dictionary at column budget C from the method's default configuration.
2. Sample exactly 4C training points, shuffled; hold out a validation split.
3. Sweep each knob one step either side of its default (factorial, <=27 cells); select on
   VALIDATION. expF03: "knobs are not additive", so factorial beats one-at-a-time.
   **TUNE ONCE per (task, method, width). Freeze the configuration, THEN run the seeds.**
   Never tune per seed -- that would let each seed pick its own best and collapse the error bars
   into a measure of the tuning grid rather than of the method.
4. Solve by truncated lstsq, rcond = 1e-15. Never form Phi^T Phi.
5. Score on the full square (masked for poisson_man).
6. Record error, selected configuration, and actual column count.

**Every derived rule is a DEFAULT and a sweep centre, never a binding constraint.** The Crowther
episode is why: a rule derived from adjacent theory was wrong by an order of magnitude and only a
five-minute measurement caught it.

| method | knobs | default | sweep |
|---|---|---|---|
| QI-Radon | lambda, halo, J/M | 0.25, min(n_ax/8,8), 1.0 | +-20%, +-2, x{0.7,1.0,1.4} |
| QI-tensor | lambda, halo | 0.25, min(n_ax/8,8) | +-20%, +-2 |
| ELM | lambda | 0.25 | +-20% |
| tensor-product spectral | none | modes/degree from C | -- |
| RBF | lambda, poly degree | 0.53, 3 | +-20%, {1,2,3} |

Equal effort per knob a method actually has. Fourier is not disadvantaged by having none.

# 15. TUNING PROTOCOLS (literature research, 2026-09-01) -- supersedes the knob table in 14.2

Shape is identical for all families: **one scalar knob -> closed-form default -> 3-point
log-spaced check on the held-out validation split -> argmin -> FREEZE, then run seeds.**
Each check point is one lstsq solve. Families 3 and 4 have no scalar knob; that is the finding,
not a gap.

## 15.1 RBF
- **Kernel: IMQ** phi(r) = (1+(eps r)^2)^{-1/2}. Positive definite, so **NO polynomial tail is
  required** and less flat-limit-fragile than the Gaussian.
- **Default eps*h = 1.** Sits between the two published closed-form rules on [-1,1]^2 with an
  n x n centre grid: Hardy (1971) gives eps*h = 1.23, Franke (1982) gives 0.57. No search needed.
- **3-point check: eps*h in {0.5, 1, 2}.**
- Rippa's LOOCV (Rippa 1999) is the canonical selector -- closed form e_k = c_k/(A^{-1})_{kk},
  one factorization per candidate, no refits; PRESS r_i/(1-H_ii) is the rectangular-lstsq
  equivalent. We do not need it at a 3-point grid, but cite it as the standard.

**CORRECTION to 13.12: RBF is NOT exempt from the no-polynomial rule.** The exemption I wrote was
based on polyharmonic splines and multiquadrics being only conditionally positive definite. IMQ is
positive definite (CPD order 0) and needs no tail, so the RBF arm runs polynomial-free like every
other method and the baseline stays uniform. Tail requirement by kernel: Gaussian/IMQ/Wendland none;
multiquadric degree 0; PHS r/r^3/r^5 degree 0/1/2; TPS r^2 log r degree 1.

**KNOWN LIMITATION, must be stated in the writeup:** rcond-truncated lstsq **cannot reach RBF's own
ceiling.** The accuracy-optimal eps lies in the flat regime where RBF-Direct is catastrophically
ill-conditioned -- and that ill-conditioning is an artifact of the BASIS, not of the interpolant,
which stays well behaved as eps -> 0. RBF-QR (Fornberg/Larsson/Flyer 2011) and Contour-Pade exist
precisely to replace truncated SVD there. So our RBF number is a lower bound on what RBF can do.
Cite it; do not try to fix it inside this benchmark.

**Second RBF entry, zero tuning: PHS(r^3) + degree-1 polynomial.** Polyharmonic splines are
scale-invariant and have NO shape parameter at all; the polynomial degree sets the convergence
order (Flyer/Fornberg/Bayona/Barnett, JCP 2016). This is the modern literature default precisely
because it removes shape-parameter tuning. Guideline: point count >= 2x the number of polynomial
terms. Recommended as a second RBF arm -- it sidesteps both the tuning question and the flat-limit
conditioning problem.

## 15.2 ELM / random features
- **Parameterization: i.i.d. W_ij, b_i ~ U(-R, R), tanh, inputs affinely mapped to [-1,1]^2.**
  This is the standard ELM/RFM convention (Dong & Yang 2022; Chen, E & Luo 2022).
- **Default R = 2.** Between RFM's published R=1 and Dong & Yang's single-hidden-layer optima of
  1.82-3.36. **3-point check: R in {1, 2, 4}.** Dong & Yang show accuracy is flat near the optimum
  and explicitly recommend rounding to a nearby "nice" value.

**CORRECTION to 14.2:** I had ELM at lambda = 0.25 via expC07. That is the wrong parameterization --
expC07's lambda is gamma*h on a UNIFORM GRID, whereas ELM draws i.i.d. weights with no grid and no h.
Published R values do not transfer between the two. Use the standard convention above and say so.

**Re-select R per width.** For a single hidden layer, the optimal R *increases* with parameter count
(Dong & Yang Fig. 2b); their near-invariance claim applies to depth >= 2. Our per-(task, method,
width) selection already handles this -- do NOT collapse it to one global R. Fix the RNG seed across
the selection run and the production run, or the 3-point comparison is noise. The literature selects
on the training residual; we use validation instead, since the training residual collapses to zero
on an underdetermined fit.

## 15.3 Tensor-product spectral
**Confirmed: literally zero hyperparameters.** No shape parameter, no scale, no initialization, no
randomness. Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto nodes x_j = cos(j*pi/N) (endpoints included so BCs are
imposable); Fourier on periodic axes, Chebyshev otherwise (Boyd; Trefethen). Mode count / degree is
fixed by the column budget. The only free choice -- collocation points per unknown -- is set
globally by expF17, so it is a benchmark-level constant, not a method knob.
**State this explicitly in the writeup** so the equal-effort rule is visibly not violated by this
arm having nothing to tune. Rectangular/least-squares reference: Driscoll & Hale 2016.
Do NOT use the Kosloff-Tal-Ezer map; it only relieves explicit time-stepping restrictions.

## 15.4 BWLer
- **Default: explicit BWLer, spectral derivatives (`fd_k=None`), CGL nodes, nodal collocation.**
- **Under a direct lstsq solve, BWLer's entire optimizer hyperparameter list vanishes** (Adam lr,
  NNCG preconditioner rank 16-1000, CG steps 16-2000) because the e^{-t/kappa^2} optimization term
  in their Theorem 5.1 is zero. So this arm is genuinely equal-effort with the others.
- 3-point check `fd_k` in {spectral, 5, 3}, run once to document then freeze. Spectral should win by
  Theorem 5.1 -- with no optimization gap the FD misspecification term N^{-(k+1-d)} is pure loss.
  The FD option only ever buys conditioning for a *gradient* optimizer.

**PARTIAL REVERSAL of 14.3.** I collapsed Fourier + Chebyshev + explicit-BWLer into one arm on the
grounds that they span the same space. The span claim is correct -- barycentric Lagrange on N+1 CGL
nodes IS P_N, and BWLer's spectral matrix IS the Chebyshev differentiation matrix -- but the
**parameterization differs** (value space vs coefficient space) and that difference is measurable:
the BWLer paper's own Chebyshev-least-squares baseline (`chebfit`, Appendix C.1.3) is beaten by
explicit BWLer *as the least-squares problem becomes more ill-conditioned* (their Fig. 4). Under
rcond-truncated lstsq that is exactly the regime we are in. **Keep both, labelled honestly as two
parameterizations of one method, not two competing methods.**

**BWLer-hat is EXCLUDED.** It is an MLP producing nodal values, hence nonlinear in its parameters,
so it cannot run under a pure lstsq solve. Including it would require gradient training and break
both the solve frame and the equal-effort rule. Run it as a separate side experiment or not at all.

**FAIRNESS NOTE, one sentence required in the writeup:** BWLer's actual contribution is about
*trainability by gradient methods*; the paper concedes its results are "not time- or
parameter-matched" and that BWLer "inherits the weaknesses of classical spectral methods, including
handling PDEs with sharp solutions or problems over irregular domains." An lstsq benchmark therefore
measures the axis on which BWLer has the *least* distinguishing content. Our result must not be
presented as a refutation of that paper.

## 17.7 Terminology (was never defined in this doc -- fixed)
**J** = number of DIRECTIONS (spokes), the angles theta_j = pi*j/J sampled around the half-circle.
**M** = number of OFFSETS per spoke, the parallel lines t_k along each direction.
Each neuron is one (theta_j, t_k) pair, so J*M ~ the neuron budget, and **J/M is how a fixed budget
splits between angular and along-ray resolution.** expH05 measured error falling exponentially in J
but only as a power law (~N^-10) in M, which is why the split is not neutral. The tensor has no
analogue -- it is forced symmetric at sqrt(N) per axis.

## 18.9 ORACLE FOOTPRINT AMENDMENT (2026-09-01, audit finding, Sam-ruled)

the 4C data were drawn from the OPEN square, so no point ever sat on the boundary
lines the 200x200 score grid contains, and the halo columns were left unpinned
there. Measured on darcy_man at W=2025 (same dictionary, seed 0): the outermost
2% band carried ~97% of the squared error (RMS 9.2e-13 vs 2.1e-14 interior);

darcy_orig were immune (they draw from the reference nodes, endpoints included).
