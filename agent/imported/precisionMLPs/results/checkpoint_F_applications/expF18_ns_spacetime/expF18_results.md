# expF18 -- 2-D unsteady Navier-Stokes on the space-time ridge mesh

**Status:** draft-pending-Sam (2026-09-07; seed 0 across the ladder, seeds 1-2 up to 2048; single machine, 16 GB).

## TL;DR

- The checkpoint-H recipe solves a real unsteady 2-D Navier-Stokes problem with **no training**: three coordinates $(x,y,t)$, one shared even direction mesh with 1-D QI blocks, three solved readouts $(u,v,p)$, damped Gauss-Newton on the stacked collocation system. At 4096 units per field the velocity is at rel $L_2$ **$1.1\times10^{-11}$** on the whole closed cube, pressure $8.6\times10^{-10}$, $\max|\nabla\cdot u|$ $6\times10^{-10}$, three Newton steps, 20 minutes.
- The error falls geometrically in width: velocity $1.8\times10^{-3}\to5.7\times10^{-6}\to8.2\times10^{-9}\to2.3\times10^{-9}\to1.1\times10^{-11}$ over 256 to 4096 units per field. The oracle fit of the exact fields on the same dictionary (the representational ceiling) is $2.8\times10^{-4}\to1.1\times10^{-12}$; the solve sits 3-10x above it at every rung except 2048 (80x).
- **The gap to the ceiling is not the solver.** At 2048 the oracle coefficients have a *higher* collocation residual ($7.8\times10^{-7}$) than the solve's minimizer ($2.1\times10^{-7}$), and Gauss-Newton started at the oracle walks to the solve's answer in one step. The collocation objective's minimizer is displaced from the best $L_2$ fit: the fit error is spiky at the cube's corners and edges ($L_\infty/L_2\approx100$) and the operator amplifies it. Same pattern as expF13's reaction task.
- The error lives at the boundary. Inside the 90% box the solve is 3x better in RMS than on the whole cube and its worst point sits on the walls and the final-time face; the interior is at $10^{-12}$ at mid-times for the top rung. Time is a coordinate, so nothing accumulates: the per-slice error is flat over $t$ with a rise only at the unconstrained final face.
- The two-floor allocation of expH06 transfers, with a twist: coarse offsets and many directions win for the oracle ($N=8$ up to 1024, 16 at 2048, 24 at 4096), but the solve wants a coarser split than the oracle once the oracle's choice is a near-tie. At 2048 the solve at $N=12$ reaches $1.2\times10^{-10}$, twenty times better than at the inherited $N=16$ ($2.3\times10^{-9}$), and only 4x above the oracle; the gap between solve and oracle grows with $N$ at every width. The weak 2048 rung of the declared ladder is this protocol misfire, not a property of the method. At 4096 the check ($N=16$ vs the inherited 24) is a wash at $9\times10^{-12}$ vs $1.1\times10^{-11}$.
- Seeds (rotations of the direction set plus fresh collocation draws) move the solve by less than 2.5x at every width up to 2048.

## Question

Does the expH06 space-time ridge mesh, with the readout solved by Gauss-Newton collocation, reach the precision the geometry admits on a genuinely nonlinear time-dependent system, incompressible Navier-Stokes in primitive variables, and how does the error scale with width?

## Experiment design

**Problem.** Unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes on the box $\Omega=[-1,1]^2$, $t\in[0,2]$, $\nu=0.01$:
$$u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p-\nu\Delta u=0,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0.$$
Exact verifier: the Taylor-Green cell $\psi=\cos(kx')\cos(ky')/k$, $k=\pi/2$ (one vortex cell per box), boosted by a uniform drift $(U,V)=(0.5,0.25)$ (Galilean invariance keeps it an exact solution): with $x'=x-Ut$, $y'=y-Vt$, $E=e^{-2\nu k^2t}$,
$$u=U-\cos(kx')\sin(ky')E,\quad v=V+\sin(kx')\cos(ky')E,\quad p=-\tfrac14\big(\cos2kx'+\cos2ky'\big)E^2 .$$
The drift matters: for the un-boosted cell $(u\cdot\nabla)u$ is a pure gradient and the Stokes step alone already solves the velocity (expF12's Beltrami had the same property); in the lab frame the curl of the advection is $O(1)$ (measured 2.3), so Newton has to do real work. The closed form is checked against the operator by finite differences (momentum $9\times10^{-10}$, divergence $3\times10^{-11}$, FD-limited). Data given to the solver: Dirichlet velocity on the four walls over time, velocity at $t=0$, and the exact pressure on one spatial point over all $t$ (the gauge line; with velocity Dirichlet data the pressure is otherwise determined only up to a function of time). Solver coordinates: the scaled cube $X=(x,y,\tau)\in[-1,1]^3$, $t=(\tau+1)$, $\partial_t=\partial_\tau$.

**Dictionary (expH06 in $d=3$).** $M$ nested even directions $v_j$ (the projective farthest-point sequence on the half sphere, `h06.core.nested_directions`), each carrying one 1-D QI block of $N$ evenly spaced offsets $t_{jl}$ on the band $[-T_j,T_j]$, $T_j=1.25\max_{\text{cube}}|v_j\cdot X|=1.25\,\|v_j\|_1$ (the cube's support function; the 25% collar is the halo), spacing $h_j=2T_j/N$, width $\gamma_j=0.25/h_j$ (the expH02 rule $\gamma h=\lambda^\star$). Features $\phi_{jl}(X)=\tanh(\gamma_j(v_j\cdot X-t_{jl}))$ plus one bias; $W=MN$ units per field. All three fields share the geometry; each has its own readout, so the unknown vector has $3(W+1)$ entries. Derivative rows are exact: $\partial^\alpha\phi=\gamma^{|\alpha|}v^\alpha\psi_{|\alpha|}(\tanh\cdot)$ with $\psi_0=\tau$, $\psi_1=1-\tau^2$, $\psi_2=-2\tau(1-\tau^2)$ (checked against finite differences to $10^{-10}$ relative, first order, and $10^{-7}$, second order).

**Oracle regime (the ceiling and the split selector).** For every width and every $N\in\{8,12,16,24,32,48\}$ with $M=\lfloor W/N\rfloor\ge4$: one truncated-SVD least squares of $(u,v,p)$ on $3(W+1)$ uniform interior points plus the six faces of the closed cube (the expF17 18.9 rule: perimeter rows pin the halo columns), rcond $10^{-15}$. The best $N$ by velocity error is inherited by the dynamic solve at that width (expF17's "dynamic arm inherits the dictionary knobs" ruling).

**Dynamic regime (the headline).** Newton-linearize the advection at the current iterate $(u^0,v^0)$; the momentum rows in the unknown increment are $[\,L+u^0\partial_x+v^0\partial_y+u^0_x\,|\,u^0_y\,|\,\partial_x\,]$ (and cyclic) with $L=\partial_\tau-\nu\Delta$; continuity $[\partial_x\,|\,\partial_y\,|\,0]$; wall, initial and gauge rows as value blocks. Each equation block is scaled to $O(1)$ by its max entry, condition blocks are weighted $w_{\rm mult}\sqrt{n_{\rm pde}/n_{\rm blk}}$ (the expF03/expF17 recipe), the stacked system $J\delta=-r$ is solved by min-norm truncated SVD at rcond $10^{-15}$ and the step is backtracked on the stacked residual norm (expF02 semantics); stop when the step is below $10^{-13}$ or the residual moves by less than $10^{-3}$ relative, at most 15 steps. Above 6000 columns the solve goes through the augmented QR: Householder QR of $[J\,|\,{-r}]$ in place with `mode='r'`, so $Q$ is never formed and $Q^\top r$ appears as the last column of $R$; then the SVD of $R$ (verified equal to the direct SVD route to $10^{-9}$ on a gapless test matrix). Peak memory at 4096 units per field is about 7 GB; the earlier route needed 12 GB and swapped. Point counts, held across the ladder: interior $n_{\rm int}=3\cdot3(W+1)$ uniform points, each wall $n_{\rm int}/16$, the initial face $n_{\rm int}/8$, 128 gauge points, i.e. 3.75 rows per unknown. Warm start: each rung fits its dictionary to the previous rung's solution (the ladder is its own cascade); the first rung starts from zero, so its first step is the Stokes solve. $w_{\rm mult}$ tuned once at 1024 over the declared grid $\{0.3,1,3\}$ with the expF17 edge walk (10 tried because 3 won; 3 kept: $1.5\times10^{-8}$, $1.4\times10^{-8}$, $8.2\times10^{-9}$, $1.2\times10^{-8}$), then inherited by every width and seed. Seeds rotate the direction set by a Haar-random rotation and redraw the collocation points; the problem never changes.

**Metrics.** Velocity rel $L_2=\|(\hat u-u,\hat v-v)\|_2/\|(u,v)\|_2$ and $L_\infty$ on a $65\times65\times41$ grid of the closed cube (both the whole cube and, in the diagnostics, the inner 90% box); pressure rel $L_2$ (raw, the gauge line fixes it); per-time-slice curves; the nonlinear NS residual (momentum RMS, $\max|\nabla\cdot u|$) at 4000 fresh interior points, computed with the model's own derivatives.

**Code & data.** `experiments/expF18_ns_spacetime/{problem.py, ridge3d.py, run.py}`; tests `tests/test_expF18_ns_spacetime.py` (6: the closed form satisfies NS and its advection is not a gradient; derivative rows vs finite differences; block rule $\gamma h=0.25$ and the cube support function; oracle convergence with width; Gauss-Newton descends from Stokes toward the oracle; the augmented-QR solve equals the SVD solve). Store `cells.json` (every cell with its per-slice curves and Newton history), coefficients `coef/*.npy`, ablations `cells_split1024.json`, `cells_split2048.json`, logs `run.log`, `split*.log`; figures in `figures/`. Reproduce: `uv run --extra dev python experiments/expF18_ns_spacetime/run.py --oracle --tune --solve --plot --gif`.

**Scaling.** Both curves descend geometrically, roughly 2.5 orders per doubling for the oracle and for the solve except at the 2048 rung, where the solve gains only 3.6x. Pressure trails velocity by about two orders throughout (its wavenumber is $2k$, and it is the constraint variable, as in expF09 and expF12). The solve's fresh momentum residual tracks the oracle fit's own residual (the oracle coefficients, differentiated, leave a momentum residual of the same size as the solve's until 4096), i.e. the residual floor at each width is set by what the dictionary can represent under the operator, not by the Newton iteration.

**Where the error sits.** On the top rung the pointwise velocity error is $10^{-12}$-$10^{-13}$ over the interior at mid-times, $10^{-11}$-$10^{-10}$ along the walls, and up to $10^{-9}$ at the corners of the final-time face. Per time slice the error is flat from $t\approx0.2$ to $1.7$ and rises toward $t=2$ (the final face carries no condition and the ridge collar ends there) and slightly at $t=0$. The oracle fit shows the same anatomy at 2048: max error $3.8\times10^{-9}$ at a corner of the cube versus $6.6\times10^{-11}$ max and $8.8\times10^{-12}$ RMS on the inner 90% box (RMS $2.4\times10^{-11}$ on the whole cube). The dynamic solve at 2048: $5.2\times10^{-8}$ max on the walls and the $t=2$ face, RMS $6.2\times10^{-10}$ inner versus $1.9\times10^{-9}$ whole.

**The gap to the ceiling is the objective, not the solver (2048 diagnostic).** Under the solve's own assembly, the oracle coefficients have stacked residual $7.8\times10^{-7}$ (PDE part $7.1\times10^{-7}$, max momentum residual $3.1\times10^{-7}$) while the converged solve has $2.1\times10^{-7}$ (max $3.8\times10^{-8}$); started from the oracle, Gauss-Newton moves to the solve's minimizer in one full step, taking the fresh velocity error from $1.6\times10^{-11}$ to $1.6\times10^{-9}$. Re-solving from the solve's answer at rcond $10^{-14}$ or $10^{-13}$ does not move (the backtracking collapses). So the least-residual point of this dictionary is a different function from its least-$L_2$-error point, by two orders in velocity: an $L_2$-tiny but spiky fit error (concentrated at corners, $L_\infty/L_2\approx100$) becomes a large operator residual once differentiated, and the collocation objective trades it away. This is the expF13 reaction-task finding ("Gauss-Newton started at the oracle walks AWAY from it, solver exonerated") on a system.

**Split.** The oracle's best $N$ moves $8\to8\to8\to16\to24$ up the ladder, expH06's alternation of directions and offsets (at 4096, $N=24$ with 170 directions beats $N=16$ with 256 by 3x and $N=8$ with 512 by 90x). The dynamic solve at 1024 over $N\in\{8,12,16,24,32\}$ gives $8.2\times10^{-9}$, $1.2\times10^{-6}$, $6.6\times10^{-6}$, $4.8\times10^{-4}$, $1.5\times10^{-2}$ against the oracle's $2.3\times10^{-9}$, $5.2\times10^{-8}$, $5.4\times10^{-7}$, $8.0\times10^{-5}$, $1.0\times10^{-2}$: the same ordering with a wider spread, so at 1024 the inherited $N=8$ is right. At 2048 it is not: the solve gives $3.9\times10^{-10}$ ($N=8$), $1.2\times10^{-10}$ ($N=12$), $2.3\times10^{-9}$ (the inherited $N=16$) where the oracle has $1.6\times10^{-10}$, $3.5\times10^{-11}$, $2.8\times10^{-11}$. The solve-to-oracle ratio rises with $N$ at every width (2-4x at $N=8$, 80x at $N=16$ for 2048): the finer along-direction grids are where the fit error is spikiest and the collocation objective pays for it. A dynamic-regime split selection over the oracle's top candidates (declared, 2-3 extra solves per width) is the protocol fix; the figure `scaling.png` carries that arm as the dotted line.

**Seeds** (up to 2048; the 4096 rung is single-seed for cost). Velocity rel $L_2$ over seeds 0/1/2: 256: $1.8/1.3/2.8\times10^{-3}$; 512: $5.7/7.7/13\times10^{-6}$; 1024: $8.2/18/20\times10^{-9}$; 2048: $2.3/2.2/4.4\times10^{-9}$. The seed range is within 2.5x everywhere, far below the width-to-width steps.

**Newton.** From the warm start every rung converges in 2-7 steps; the first rung's first step is the Stokes solve (velocity $0.4$, pressure $O(1)$ wrong, as physics demands with the drift), and the second step recovers the flow to $4\times10^{-2}$. At 1024 the convergence after step 3 is linear and slow (1-2% per step), the signature of a truncated-SVD step on a rank-deficient system (rank 3045 of 3075); at 4096, 3376 of 12243 columns survive the truncation of the oracle fit and 3 steps suffice.

## Additional details

- **Cost.** One Gauss-Newton step is one dense least squares; at 4096 units per field that is $46\,037\times12\,243$, about 5 minutes on the M4 (Householder QR without forming $Q$, then the SVD of the $12\,243^2$ factor). Assembling the derivative feature matrices is a further 2-3 minutes. The ladder to 4096 is about 25 minutes wall for one seed; the oracle scan over 30 (width, $N$) cells is 3 minutes.
- **Memory.** Storing the condition blocks compactly (one field block each) and the augmented-QR solve brought the 4096 rung from about 12 GB (which swapped and stalled at one core) to about 7 GB peak. 8192 units per field (24.6k unknowns, 92k rows) would need about 25 GB and is out of reach here.
- **Split at 4096 (dynamic).** The oracle chose $N=24$ (170 directions); the solve at $N=16$ (256 directions, `cells_split4096.json`) gives velocity $9.1\times10^{-12}$ and pressure $1.0\times10^{-9}$ against $1.1\times10^{-11}$ and $8.6\times10^{-10}$ at $N=24$: a wash. Both sit 8-10x above the oracle's $1.1\times10^{-12}$, so at the top rung the gap is the objective displacement, not the split. Five Newton steps at 5 minutes each; the fresh-point velocity error is $5.7\times10^{-12}$ (the whole-cube number carries the corners).
- **Scoring region.** All headline numbers are on the closed cube including walls, corners and both time faces, the honest measure for a PDE solve with Dirichlet data. expH06 scored the inner 90% ball; on that convention the whole-cube numbers above improve by 3x in RMS and 50-60x in max.
- **Not done.** Only seed 0 reaches 4096; the rows-per-column ratio, the collar width (1.25), $\lambda=0.25$ and rcond $10^{-15}$ are the repo defaults and were not swept here; no learned directions or atoms (expH06) were used, although the velocity is an exact sum of two space-time ridges times a slow exponential and the pressure of two more, which is exactly the structure the atom machinery finds.

## Conclusions

Pending Sam. What the data supports without qualification: the frozen space-time ridge mesh with a solved readout reaches $10^{-11}$ velocity error on unsteady 2-D Navier-Stokes with three Newton steps and no training, the error scales geometrically with width, the fit ceiling of the same dictionary is $10^{-12}$ at 4096 units, and the remaining gap is a property of the collocation objective (its minimizer is displaced from the best fit) rather than of the Gauss-Newton solver.

## Open questions

- Closing the objective gap: row equilibration of the PDE block (the F13 lead), or scoring/weighting that stops the corner spikes from dominating; and whether a collar or direction set adapted to the cube's corners removes the spikes at the source.
- Atoms for the ridge structure: the exact solution is four space-time ridges (up to the slow decay); projection pursuit plus the Gauss-Newton polish should find them and reach the floor at a fraction of 4096 units, as it did for expH06's ridge sums.
- Reynolds number: $\nu=0.01$ converged without continuation from the ladder's warm start; where plain Newton stops and whether $\nu$-continuation (expF06) rescues it is untested.
