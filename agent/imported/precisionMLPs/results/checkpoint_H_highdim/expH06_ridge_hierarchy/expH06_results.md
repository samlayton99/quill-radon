# expH06: the hierarchical ridge mesh in d = 3, 4

**Status:** draft-pending-Sam (single seed for the hierarchy and the even cells; three seeds for the ridge recovery; nothing committed).

## TL;DRw

- Hidden ridge directions can be learned exactly. Sums of 1, 2, 4, 8 hidden ridges in $d=3$ and $d=4$ (3 seeds each, 24 cases) are recovered to $2$-$10\times10^{-13}$ with the directions exact to fp64, by projection pursuit on the residual followed by a joint Gauss-Newton polish of all directions with the readout eliminated. The polish is the step that matters: projection pursuit alone leaves 20-80% of the residual per atom.
- The two-floor law holds in 3-D (46 of 48 exact cells within 10% of $\max(e_M,e_N)$), directions bind, and the coarse-$N$ split wins: at 4096 units, 256-512 directions with 8-16 offsets beat 64-128 directions with 32-64. The even nested-direction mesh reaches the fp64 floor in 3-D on the $r=0.3$ ball on four of six targets at 9216 units ($M=192$, $N=48$): fast waves $8\times10^{-13}$, composition $2\times10^{-13}$, gauss $2\times10^{-14}$, product sines $9\times10^{-14}$; radial Runge $1.4\times10^{-12}$ at $(320,24)$.
- Learning every direction of an even mesh by the same Gauss-Newton polish buys 5-125x on directions-bound cells (3-D radial Runge $10^{-8}\to10^{-10}$, packet $10^{-5}\to10^{-7}$; 4-D ridge4+bump $7\times10^{-4}\to10^{-8}$) and nothing on offsets-bound ones, as the law predicts; on the already-grown meshes it buys 1.3-6x.
- The greedy hierarchy (nested coarse background + atoms + refine-vs-open by trial fits) reaches the floor at a fraction of the even budget on every target with ridge structure (ridge2 $2\times10^{-14}$ at 160 units, ridge4 $4\times10^{-14}$ at 992, product sines $2\times10^{-14}$ at 1088, ridge4+bump $8\times10^{-14}$ at 3420) and beats the even mesh at 4096 on the smooth 3-D targets (composition $6\times10^{-13}$ vs $6\times10^{-11}$; gauss $5\times10^{-13}$ vs $4\times10^{-12}$; fast waves $4\times10^{-11}$ vs $5\times10^{-10}$). In 4-D at 4096 units: ridge4+bump $9\times10^{-12}$ (even: $4\times10^{-6}$), gauss $7\times10^{-11}$ (even $5\times10^{-10}$), composition $1.6\times10^{-9}$ (even $2.9\times10^{-9}$), radial Runge $1.5\times10^{-7}$ (even $3.7\times10^{-7}$).
- With rcond $10^{-14}$ the even mesh at $(256,48)$ is at the floor on four of six targets (fast waves $3\times10^{-13}$, composition $3\times10^{-14}$, gauss $10^{-14}$, product sines $6\times10^{-14}$); the $10^{-13}$ threshold from 1-D was cutting needed modes at these widths.
- End state, 3-D on the $r=0.3$ ball (single seed): gauss $1.3\times10^{-14}$, composition $2.7\times10^{-14}$, product sines $5.9\times10^{-14}$ (all even $(256,48)$ at rcond $10^{-14}$); radial Runge $9.6\times10^{-14}$ (even $(320,32)$ + all-directions polish); fast waves $3.0\times10^{-13}$; spatial packet $3.3\times10^{-12}$ (even $(384,32)$ + polish -- at $M=256$ it sits at $5.5\times10^{-9}$ at every rcond, so the packet was direction count, not truncation). Every 3-D target is within $3.3\times10^{-12}$ of the fp64 floor; the greedy hierarchy remains the cheap route on ridge-structured targets and the balanced even split + rcond $10^{-14}$ + polish is the route on isotropic ones.

## Question

Can a first layer for $d=3,4$ be built (and its directions learned) so that one truncated-SVD least squares on the readout reaches the fp64 floor on a data ball, using (i) a nested uniform background of coarse 1-D QI blocks, (ii) atoms, i.e. directions found from the data, and (iii) an allocation between opening directions and refining them that follows the two-floor law?

## Experiment design

Everything is centered on the data origin $x_0$ (expH04/H05 convention: $x_0=(0.35,-0.25,0.2)$ in 3-D, $(0.35,-0.25,0.2,-0.1)$ in 4-D), data uniform in the ball of radius $r=0.3$ about $x_0$, error scored as relative $L_2$ on 20000 fresh points in the inner ball of radius $0.9r$ (the collar is never scored).

**Block.** One direction $v$ (unit) carries $n$ evenly spaced offsets $t_j=-T+(j+\tfrac12)h$ on the projected data's band $T=1.25\max_i|v\cdot z_i|$, $h=2T/n$, and the width $\gamma=0.25/h$ (the expH02 rule $\gamma h=\lambda^\star$). Feature $\tanh(\gamma(v\cdot z-t_j))$, $z=x-x_0$. Within a block the spacing is even, so the mesh-map smoothness limit (no content below 12 gaps, expH04) is satisfied by construction; refining a block replaces its grid by a finer even grid. The readout is one truncated-SVD least squares on $[\Phi,\mathbf 1]$ at rcond $10^{-13}$.

**Nested background directions.** A projective farthest-point sequence from a spread pool (spherical Fibonacci in 3-D, 4096 normalized Gaussians in 4-D): every prefix is an evenly spread set and is contained in the next. At $M=64$ the nested set is 2-4x better than the expH01 Fibonacci set; at $M=128$ they are equal.

**Atoms (learned directions).** Projection pursuit: for each of 600 (3-D) or 1500 (4-D) pool directions fit one even QI block of 32 offsets along it to the current residual and score the fraction of the residual left; polish the best three by Gauss-Newton on the direction alone; keep the winner. Then the joint polish: with $\delta_i\in\mathbb R^{d-1}$ the tangent coordinates of direction $i$ ($v_i=\mathrm{normalize}(v_i^0+U_i\delta_i)$), residual $r(\delta)=y-A(\delta)\,c(\delta)$ where $c$ is the truncated-SVD readout, Kaufman's variable-projection Jacobian $J_{ik}=-P^\perp\,(\partial A/\partial\delta_{ik})\,c$ with $\partial A/\partial\delta_{ik}=\gamma\,\mathrm{sech}^2(\cdot)\,(ZU_{ik})$ on the block's columns and $P^\perp$ projecting off the kept left singular vectors; damped Gauss-Newton steps (halve until the residual drops), the readout re-solved at every trial. Near a zero-residual optimum (a ridge sum with exact directions) this converges quadratically. In the ridge-recovery mode the atoms are pulled one at a time from the residual, then all directions are polished jointly (20 iterations); if the residual is still above $10^{-10}$, more atoms are pulled (up to $2r$).

**The greedy hierarchy.** Start from nothing. Each round, build every candidate that fits the budget, fit it, and score it by the drop in $\log_{10}$ of the validation error (a held-out eighth of the data, inside the ball) per unit added; apply the best:
- `open_bg`: the next batch of nested directions (25% of the current units, at least 4 directions), each a block of $N_0=8$ offsets;
- `open_atom`: one direction from projection pursuit on the residual with $N_{\rm atom}=32$ offsets, then all atoms polished jointly (4 iterations on a row subsample of $\max(6000,4\cdot\text{units})$); offered only if that single direction removes at least 7% of the residual on its own (atoms accepted on a diffuse residual were found to remove 4% and to stall the path);
- `refine_bg`: every background block $N\to\lceil1.5N\rceil$, or the longest prefix in insertion order that fits the budget;
- `refine_atoms`: every atom block $N\to2N$ (same prefix rule).

Stop at the budget (4096), at $3\times10^{-13}$, or after three rounds with under 10% improvement. Nothing installed is moved or deleted; the approximation space only grows. Training set 21504 points (+3072 validation).

**Even references.** The same nested directions with a uniform $N$: (a) budgets 128-4096 at $N\in\{8,16,32\}$ (best split per budget is the dashed line in the trajectory figure); (b) in 3-D the two floor curves $e_M(M)$ at $N=48$ ($M=4$-$160$) and $e_N(N)$ at $M=128$ ($N=4$-$64$), eight exact $(M,N)$ cells for the max-of-two-floors test, two Fibonacci cells, and push cells $(256,16)$, $(320,24)$, $(384,20)$, $(512,16)$, $(256,32)$, $(192,48)$, $(256,48)$; one fixed training set of 32768 points, six targets solved against one SVD.

**Targets** (3-D unless noted; radial ones use $\rho=\|x-a\|/\sqrt2$ with $a$ inside the ball): gauss bump $e^{-\|x-a_1\|^2/0.5^2}$; radial Runge $1/(1+16\rho^2)$; fast waves $\cos 6\pi\rho$; composition $\exp(\sin\pi x_1\cos\pi x_2+0.5\cos(\pi x_3+1))$ (genuinely 3-D); product sines $\prod_k\sin2\pi x_k$ (an exact sum of four ridges in 3-D); spatial packet $0.8e^{-(\rho_0/0.18)^2}\cos10\pi\rho_0+\text{bump}$ at the ball center; hidden ridge sums $\sum_{i=1}^r\sin(\omega_i\,u_i\cdot z+\phi_i)$ with random unit $u_i$, $\omega_i\in[8,20]$, optionally plus $0.3\times$ the gauss bump (`ridge4_bump`).

**Metrics.** rel $L_2=\|\hat f-f\|_2/\|f\|_2$ on the inner ball; direction error $\arccos|v\cdot u|$ against the nearest true ridge direction.

**Code & data.** `experiments/expH06_ridge_hierarchy/{run.py, modes.py, viz.py}`, package `h06/{core, targets, atoms, grow}.py`; tests `tests/test_expH06_ridge_hierarchy.py` (7: block rule $\gamma h=0.25$, nesting, exact directions at the floor, Jacobian vs finite differences, single-ridge recovery, 4-ridge joint polish, grower on 2 ridges within 256 units). Data: `floors_d3.json`, `push_d3.json`, `push_d3_big.json`, `ridges.json`, `grow_d3_{A,A2,B,B2}.json` (A2/B2 supersede the same targets in A/B), `grow_d4_A.json`, `polish_d3.json`; logs `log_*.txt`; figures in `figures/`.

## Results

**Ridge recovery.** All 24 cases at $2$-$10\times10^{-13}$; direction errors $0$ to $2\times10^{-8}$ rad except three $r=8$ seeds where one polished direction stuck (0.25-0.64 rad) and one or two extra atoms pulled from the residual covered it. Time 3-30 s (3-D), 8-70 s (4-D), on 12000 points. Before the joint polish the stagewise residual is $10^{-2}$-$10^{-1}$; after it, $10^{-12}$.

**Two floors in 3-D.** $e_M$ falls steadily through $M=160$ on every target; $e_N$ at $M=128$ is flat from $N=24$ to $64$ on every target (it is capped by $e_M(128)$). Measured/predicted on the exact cells: 0.96-1.01 in 46 of 48, the two exceptions ($1.8$, $1.9$) being near-floor gauss cells. The best even cells: fast waves, composition, gauss, product sines at the floor at $(192,48)$; radial Runge $1.4\times10^{-12}$ at $(320,24)$; packet $3\times10^{-10}$ at $(320,24)$. The $(256,48)$ cell (12288 columns on 32768 rows, 2.7 rows per column) is worse than $(192,48)$ on four targets, a rows-per-column effect, not a law.

The ridge-structured targets are solved by atoms early (the first rounds are atoms, the even mesh never reaches $10^{-9}$); the smooth diffuse targets are built from the background (gauss takes no atom at all; composition takes 19 atoms that pass the 7% gate). On radial Runge the greedy path refines all blocks in one $\times1.5$ step at 187 directions and then can only add $N=8$ directions, ending with a mixture of $N=18$ and $N=8$ blocks at $5\times10^{-10}$ validation; the balanced even cell $(256,16)$ is 20x better. On the packet neither construction is close.

**4-D, budget 4096.** ridge4+bump $9.1\times10^{-12}$ (452 dirs, 20 atoms; even $3.6\times10^{-6}$); gauss $7.5\times10^{-11}$ (400 dirs, 10 atoms; even $4.5\times10^{-10}$ at $(512,8)$); composition $1.6\times10^{-9}$ (455 dirs, 19 atoms; even $2.9\times10^{-9}$); radial Runge $1.5\times10^{-7}$ (482 dirs, 6 atoms; even $3.7\times10^{-7}$). In 4-D the even best split is $N=8$ at every budget: coarse offsets, many directions.

**Learning all directions (variable projection, 10-12 Gauss-Newton iterations, $M(d-1)$ parameters).** On even meshes that are directions-bound the gain is large: 3-D $(128,32)$ radial Runge $1.0\times10^{-8}\to1.1\times10^{-10}$, composition $1.1\times10^{-10}\to4.7\times10^{-12}$, packet $1.2\times10^{-5}\to9.6\times10^{-8}$, fast waves 5x, product sines 2.5x, gauss unchanged at its floor; 4-D $(256,16)$ ridge4+bump $6.7\times10^{-4}\to1.2\times10^{-8}$ (the polish finds the hidden ridges from a uniform start), composition 50x, radial Runge 28x, gauss 12x. On offsets-bound meshes it does nothing (3-D $(256,16)$: radial Runge $1.54\to1.51\times10^{-11}$), as the two-floor law says. On the grown meshes, which the greedy path already tuned and which end offsets-bound (background at 8-18 offsets), the gain is 1.3-2.6x in 3-D (radial Runge $3.1\to1.7\times10^{-10}$, packet $2.3\to0.9\times10^{-8}$) and 2-6x in 4-D (composition $1.6\times10^{-9}\to2.8\times10^{-10}$, gauss $7.5\to1.7\times10^{-11}$, radial Runge $1.5\to0.8\times10^{-7}$); directions move by a median of 0.5-13 degrees, a few redundant ones swing 60-90 degrees with no effect. 3-40 minutes per target at 4096 units.

**Larger even cells with a QR-first solve** (49152 rows): $(320,32)$ and $(384,32)$ give radial Runge $1.5\times10^{-12}$, composition $8.6\times10^{-12}$, gauss $10^{-11}$, packet $5\times10^{-11}$ at 12288 units; $(256,48)$ with 49152 rows reproduces the 32768-row numbers (fast waves $1.2\times10^{-11}$, worse than $(192,48)$), so that uptick is not a rows-per-column effect. With rcond $10^{-14}$ the big cells close the last targets: radial Runge $(320,32)$ starts at $7.4\times10^{-13}$ and the polish takes it to $9.6\times10^{-14}$ (train $8.7\times10^{-14}$, directions move at most $6.8^\circ$); the packet $(384,32)$ starts at $4.0\times10^{-12}$ and polishes to $3.3\times10^{-12}$. A check cell $(320,40)$ confirms the optima are per-target: there the packet is $1.5\times10^{-10}$ (it needs $M\ge384$) and fast waves $8\times10^{-12}$ (it needs $N=48$); no single cell is best for all six. It is the truncation threshold: one QR+SVD per cell evaluated at rcond $10^{-12}$-$10^{-16}$ shows that at $(256,48)$ rcond $10^{-14}$ gives fast waves $3.0\times10^{-13}$, composition $2.7\times10^{-14}$, gauss $1.3\times10^{-14}$, product sines $5.9\times10^{-14}$ (vs $1.2\times10^{-11}$, $1.4\times10^{-12}$, $1.6\times10^{-13}$, $3.1\times10^{-13}$ at $10^{-13}$), with no degradation down to $10^{-16}$ and readout norms that stay $O(1)$ at $10^{-14}$ (they reach $10^{3}$-$10^{4}$ at $10^{-16}$ on the smaller cell without hurting the test error). The $10^{-13}$ threshold inherited from 1-D cuts needed modes once the dictionary is wide and redundant ($s_{\max}$ grows with $M$). Radial Runge ($1.1\times10^{-11}$) and the packet ($5.5\times10^{-9}$) do not move with rcond at $M=256$: they are direction-bound.

**Where the error sits (2-D diagnostic, `spikes/`).** With 64 offsets per spoke and $M=6$-$16$ on the $r=0.4$ ball, the pointwise error is a fine, nearly isotropic ripple over the whole disk; the median error at the spoke angles and midway between spokes agree within 2x at every $M$ and every radius. The direction error is not concentrated in the angular gaps.

**4-D floors and the direction-heavy push** (rcond $10^{-14}$, 32768 rows, QR-first solve). The two-floor law carries to 4-D (exact cells at 0.97-1.00 of $\max(e_M,e_N)$). The offsets floor saturates by $N=12$ on every target in the measured error range -- at $M=384$, $N=6$ already gives composition $9.9\times10^{-9}$ at 2304 units -- so essentially the whole 4-D budget belongs in directions. $e_M$ falls about 1.3-1.5 orders per doubling of $M$ (3-D gave $\approx2.5$): the $(kr)^{d-1}$ tax made visible. Direction-heavy cells at the memory wall: at $(1024,12)$, 12288 units, composition $4.5\times10^{-11}$, gauss $3.8\times10^{-11}$, radial Runge $2.1\times10^{-9}$, product sines $1.1\times10^{-9}$, fast waves $7.9\times10^{-9}$, packet $1.5\times10^{-6}$; several targets are offsets-bound there ($N=8$ costs an order on the wave targets), putting the 4-D floor crossing near $(1536$-$2048, 16$-$24)\approx25$-$50$k units -- beyond the dense solve on this machine. 4-D to the floor therefore requires packing, not scale.

Readings: sequential atoms beat the even mesh 3-30x at equal cost everywhere informative; spikes' first win is fast waves at 4096 ($1.0\times10^{-11}$, 36x over even) and they are otherwise a wash; water-filling pays only where profile bandwidth varies by direction (fast waves 3x) and is harmful on the packet at 4096; sequential re-aiming at the updated residual is essential (a batched scan of one residual earned nothing, measured). The fast-waves result puts that target within reach of its floor at half the units of its even crossing ($4096$ vs $9216$). The recalibrated off-center target (width 0.10, freq $8\pi$, `alloc_d3_off2.json`) is in range and is a clean negative for both learned arms: at 4096, even $2.9\times10^{-7}$ beats atoms $3.8\times10^{-7}$, spikes $7.2\times10^{-7}$, waterfill $3.1\times10^{-6}$ (at 2048 all four tie at $1.8\times10^{-4}$). A spatially localized radial feature still radiates energy across all directions, so uniform coverage serves it best. **Step-2 verdict on offset grading: at uniform data it has not earned its complexity anywhere** -- the single spike "win" (fast waves 4096) is within noise of the uniform-atom arm. What pays at uniform data is learned directions (atoms, polish). Spikes' design regime -- genuinely concentrated profiles -- is expected only when the data itself is non-uniform (step 3 of the program), and they should be retried there, not here.

## Additional details

- The greedy score (log-gain per unit) prefers small steps; without the 7% gate it kept accepting 32-unit atoms that explained nothing on the gauss bump and stalled at $7\times10^{-9}$. Two other defects fixed during the run: a stall rule that quit at $6\times10^{-12}$ while still descending, and a refine step that was dropped near the budget cap because it did not fit (now a prefix is refined). The A2/B2 files are the reruns with all three fixes; A/B keep the earlier runs of the same targets.
- Rank at the large cells is far below the column count (e.g. 3118 of 8193 at $(512,16)$): the union of many 1-D blocks is heavily redundant at low degree and the truncated SVD absorbs it; this is why 512 directions with 8-16 offsets remain a well-posed solve.
- The ridge-recovery time and the atom step scale with the pool size ($600$-$1500$ one-block solves on 6000 points) plus a handful of full SVDs per polish; the hierarchy's rounds are dominated by the 3-4 trial fits at the current width.

## Conclusions

Pending Sam. What the data supports without qualification: a direction that carries a ridge can be learned to fp64 exactness by variable-projection Gauss-Newton from a coarse projection-pursuit start, in 3-D and 4-D, and the two-floor law and coarse-$N$ allocation carry over to 3-D, where the even nested mesh reaches the floor at about 9000 units on four of six targets.

## Open questions

- Allocation on isotropic targets: the greedy path cannot land on the balanced split; the fix is to use the two floors directly (measure $e_M$ and $e_N$ on cheap cells, take the crossing) and let the greedy machinery handle atoms only.
- The localized packet (high frequency on a small region): both constructions fail at 4096; this is the case where a reduced radius (a second origin, or a gate) would cut the direction count.
- Whether the budget for the 3-D floor can be brought under 4096 by combining the balanced split, the all-directions polish (which buys 5-90x on directions-bound cells) and atoms; and how far the polish goes with more iterations or a fresh nested batch after it.
